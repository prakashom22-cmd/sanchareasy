How this calculator sizes your inverter and battery

Most buying mistakes happen because people size by watts alone. We work through four steps and show them under “See the working”:

  • Running load. Add the watts of everything used together.
  • Inverter size. Load ÷ 0.8 (never run an inverter above 80%) ÷ 0.8 power factor, then the next standard VA. Fans, fridges and pumps pull a heavy surge at start, so that is checked too.
  • System voltage. Up to 1,100 VA uses 12 V, up to 2,500 VA uses 24 V, above that 48 V. Higher voltage means lower current and safer wiring.
  • Battery Ah. Energy needed, plus inverter loss, plus the depth of discharge you can safely use, plus a cut for fast discharge (Peukert effect) and ageing.

Five mistakes to avoid

  • Using watts × hours ÷ volts alone. It under-sizes the battery.
  • Buying the biggest inverter “to be safe” without matching the battery. A large inverter on a small battery drains it fast.
  • Connecting batteries of different age, brand or Ah together.
  • Trying to run an AC or geyser on a home inverter.
  • Believing the headline Ah. Ask the dealer for Ah at your backup hours (for example the 5-hour rate).

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate inverter size for my home?

Add up the watts of everything you want to run together. Divide by 0.8 so the inverter is never loaded above 80%, then divide by the power factor (0.8) to get VA, and pick the next standard size. Also check that the inverter can handle the startup surge of fans, pumps and fridges.

How many batteries do I need for my backup time?

It depends on load and hours. Example: about 470 W (4 LED bulbs, 2 tube lights, 3 fans, a TV, a router and a laptop) for 3 hours needs an 850 VA inverter with two 12 V 180 Ah tubular batteries.

Tubular or lithium battery for an inverter?

Tubular lead-acid costs less upfront but should only be discharged about 50% for a long life and needs water top-ups. Lithium (LiFePO4) costs more, gives about 80% usable capacity, is lighter and needs no maintenance, so it can work out cheaper over its life for heavy daily use.

Why is watts × hours ÷ volts not enough?

That formula ignores inverter losses, the fact that lead-acid batteries deliver less than their headline Ah at faster discharge (Peukert effect), and the depth of discharge you can safely use. It usually under-sizes the battery.

Can a home inverter run an AC or geyser?

Usually not on a normal home setup. Air conditioners and geysers draw thousands of watts and need a very large inverter and battery bank, which is rarely practical. Plan inverters for lights, fans, TV, Wi-Fi, laptop and fridge.
